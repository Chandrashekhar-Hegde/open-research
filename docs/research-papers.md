# Research papers

Write from completed records. A paper is a report of work; selecting a format does not conduct the study.

This is a practical starting path. Confirm the methods, local requirements and
current venue instructions for your actual study; this is not an exhaustive standard.

## Choose your route

- Original empirical work: organize around the research question, actual methods, findings and interpretation.
- Theory or artifact work: adapt the outline to definitions/derivation or design/evaluation; keep the claim-to-evidence link.
- If work has not been executed, write a protocol or proposal and label it explicitly.

## Records you need

- Question and protocol history, actual methods, evidence and claims ledgers, and outputs.
- Limitations, deviations, negative results, contributions, funding/conflicts and data/code access.

## Study flow

1. Audit which claims the evidence supports.
2. Prepare methods and result tables from records.
3. Draft the introduction and discussion around those findings.
4. Write an accurate abstract, check every citation and complete applicable reporting guidance.
5. Apply the venue format and obtain the necessary author and methodological review.

## Analysis checks

- Trace every quantitative sentence to an output and its denominator/uncertainty.
- Do not turn planned work or literature background into observed results.

## Reporting and formatting

- Use meaningful headings and consistent citation style; distinguish tables, figures and supplementary records.
- The Write panel exports Markdown or Quarto (.qmd). Quarto can render HTML or Word locally; journal templates and reference styles are separate choices. No automatic submission or journal certification is supplied.

## What counts as done

The exported file is an authoring scaffold containing your recorded text. It remains a draft until an accountable author verifies its claims, sources and reporting.
