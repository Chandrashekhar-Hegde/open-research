Review papers

A review is a study of existing evidence. Its search, selection and synthesis are part of the method.

Practical starting guidance. Confirm methods, local requirements and current venue instructions for your actual study.

Choose your route

  • Systematic review: answer a focused question with explicit eligibility, reproducible search and critical appraisal.
  • Scoping review: map the extent and characteristics of evidence; use the appropriate PRISMA extension.
  • Narrative review: explain scope and source-selection limits; do not label it systematic or exhaustive without the corresponding method.
  • Meta-analysis is optional: pool only scientifically compatible estimates with a justified statistical model.

Records you need

  • Protocol and changes, databases and complete searches with dates, deduplication and screening records.
  • Eligibility, excluded full texts with reasons, extracted data and source locations.
  • Appraisal method, disagreements, synthesis decisions, missing evidence and limitations.

Study flow

  1. Define the review question and check whether an update or new synthesis is needed.
  2. Specify eligibility, search, screening, extraction and appraisal before selection.
  3. Run searches and document the flow of records; resolve screening/extraction differences.
  4. Appraise and synthesize the included evidence without hiding disagreement.
  5. Report the process, results, certainty limitations and any updates to the protocol.

Analysis checks

  • Check duplicate cohorts, incompatible outcomes, selective reporting and dependence before pooling.
  • Count studies separately from reports. A long reference list is neither a search method nor evidence certainty.

Reporting and formatting

  • For systematic reviews, use PRISMA 2020 and the relevant extension; include the flow diagram, included-study table and complete search strategy.
  • Use a structured Methods and Synthesis section. Narrative reviews should state selection limitations; avoid implying all relevant evidence was found. The review scaffold prompts these records without inventing them.

What counts as done?

A review can be original research without new experiments. It needs an executed, inspectable evidence-selection and synthesis process.
